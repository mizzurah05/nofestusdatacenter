Late 2025: Backroom Deals and Secret Rezoning

  • Quiet Collusion: City officials and developers coordinate behind closed doors on a massive hyperscale project, later exposed through public records and leaked text messages showing leadership dismissing concerned residents.
  • November 27, 2025: The Festus City Council quietly pushes through a vote to rezone roughly 360 acres of land (north of Highway 67 and west of Highway CC) to clear the way for the CRG/Clayco development.
  • Winter 2025: Residents catch wind of the project and begin organizing resistance against the development footprint.

March 2026: The Showdown Vote

  • March 30, 2026: Hundreds of furious residents pack a local high school gym for a hostile public hearing. Despite overwhelming public outcry over water use, energy grid strain, and secrecy, the City Council votes 6–2 to approve Bill No. 4876, locking in the development agreement with CRG.

April 2026: The Ballot Box Rejection and Legal Action

  • April 7, 2026: Total Electoral Reversal. Festus holds its municipal election. Voters oust every single incumbent city council member seeking reelection who voted in favor of the data center. Another council member resigns under the mounting pressure. The historic flip makes national news.
  • Throughout April: Alongside the election, angry residents blanket polling places collecting signatures for a sweeping recall petition targeting Mayor Sam Richards and remaining pro-development council members.
  • April 20, 2026: Local property owners file a formal lawsuit in St. Louis County against the City of Festus and CRG, targeting the shady rezoning process and severe violations of the Missouri Sunshine Law.

June 2026: Recall Deflections and Damage Control

  • June 8, 2026: The City Council votes on resolutions regarding the citizen recall petitions. Backed by city legal counsel arguing the petitions technically only signaled policy disagreements rather than formal unlawful conduct, the council blocks the recall measures from heading to a public vote.
  • Late June 2026: Stung by intense legal pressure and total political fallout, developers are forced to scale back their initial ambitions, offering a "refined" conceptual design that shrinks the footprint and adds buffer zones—though community opposition remains entirely unbroken.
